## What was used

- ESCO: skills, skill groups (pillar roots excluded), broader relations, the transversal skills
  collection. French labels: yes.
  Occupations: their descriptions only, as part of the common-word corpus (below); the
  occupation-skill relations are downloaded but not used.
- O*NET: Technology Skills, Tools Used (examples and their UNSPSC commodity classes), Occupation Data,
  Alternate Titles, Sample of Reported Titles, Job Zones, Knowledge; Task Statements only as part of
  the common-word corpus.
- Military crosswalk: active primary codes (Army, Marine Corps, Navy, Air Force, Space Force, Coast Guard) mapped to O*NET-SOC; enlisted AFSCs also under their skill-level-X form (3D052 -> 3D0X2).
- NAICS 2022: 2-6 digit codes; the sector ranges 31-33, 44-45 and 48-49 are expanded to each code.
- This build writes an empty IDF and the curated related edges only; scripts/compute_idf.py and
  scripts/compute_pmi.py compute IDF, the mined edges and the common words from the posting corpus
  and add the "Corpus statistics" section below (run them after every build).

## Rules worth knowing when reading the artifacts

- Aliases (DECISIONS D-26): an ESCO concept merged into a curated node brings its preferred labels,
  and, into a skill (never a field of study), an alternative or hidden label of several words that
  shares a specific content word with the curated names or is made of the curated label's own
  words ("control system"; 827 kept); a
  one-word French preferred label is kept only when it is the English label. ESCO programming
  languages (labelled "(computer programming)" or under computer programming) are TECHNOLOGY.
  The formal in-progress designations of a professional licence or of the CFA charter ("CPA
  candidate") never reach it, and the curated posting probes keep their licence (DECISIONS D-28).
- titles.json: a primary O*NET title maps to its occupation; an alternate or reported title claimed
  by several occupations goes to the SOC major group most of them share (ties: the smaller group
  code), then to that group's smallest code. Trailing parentheticals give two keys
  (`CEO (Chief Executive Officer)` -> `ceo`, `chief executive officer`). Job zone null: O*NET has none.
  Generic titles (curated `generic_titles`: intern, co-op, student, trainee, apprentice, volunteer,
  engineer, analyst, ...) name no occupation and are left out.
- mos_crosswalk.json: one entry per code; `branches` lists every service using it, `branch` is the
  first in Army, Marine Corps, Navy, Air Force, Space Force, Coast Guard order, and `alternatives`
  holds a service whose use of the code maps elsewhere. `skill_ids` (DECISIONS D-29) come only from
  the occupations a code maps to: their hot or in-demand technologies and top five knowledge areas
  (importance >= 3), never fields of study and never a knowledge area more than 25%
  of all codes would get (`common_knowledge_left_out`). An entry without skills carries a `note`.
- Codes without skill ids: 1,827 of 8,385 codes have none: 1,023 map only to military-specific occupations (SOC 55-xxxx), 804 to civilian occupations O*NET publishes no skill data for ("All Other" catch-alls and the like), 0 to occupations whose only data was left out.
- Common words: lower-case words used at least 2 times in the ESCO skill and
  occupation descriptions and the O*NET occupation descriptions and task statements. A shortened
  source name (vendor-free, without "software", a bare label, an acronym half) that is one such
  word is not an alias (`Microsoft Paint` keeps no `Paint`).
- resolver.json (DECISIONS D-26, D-27): `ambiguous` holds the curated keys that need their casing
  (a skills list waives it); `cased` holds, automatically, every one-word name with a capital on a
  node that is not curated (O*NET products and tools, ESCO languages, acronyms), cased as written
  everywhere: lower-case text never resolves to them and the fuzzy fallback never targets them.
  `blocked_phrases` ("American Express", "Sous Chef", "Building C", "Technical Skills")
  consume their words in extraction without a mention. `in_progress` maps each professional
  licence, and the CFA charter, to the node of its formal in-progress designations, read only as
  written (D-28). `currency_skill_nouns` (curated) keep a currency code that is also a skill (CAD)
  the skill after an amount ("1,200 CAD drawings"); before any other word it is money (D-27).

## Corpus statistics

Written by `scripts/compute_idf.py` and `scripts/compute_pmi.py` (Task 2.3; SPEC §3.1-§3.2; DECISIONS
D-21, D-22) from Tailrd's own postings. Aggregates only: the posting text stays in `data/raw/corpus/`
(git-ignored, D-04). `corpus_report.md` lists the most frequent ids, the strongest mined edges and the
noisy-alias review.

- Source: Tailrd scraped postings (scraped_jobs), Neon Development branch: the postings whose
  description is longer than 200 characters and that are no duplicate (`duplicate_of IS NULL`), pulled
  by `scripts/pull_corpus.py` on 2026-10-09.
- 5,473 postings; first seen 2026-05-12 to 2026-05-14; scraped 2026-05-12 to 2026-05-14;
  `postings.jsonl.gz` sha256 `bd943d2d851ee2680124cd6bea3c98cea77e3b73d01c3e784722300f03e30bac`.
- Extraction: each posting is read as its title, a line break and its description, decoded from HTML
  (entities, then tags; a block tag ends a line), through `Resolver.extract` in prose context over this
  taxonomy. A skill counts once per posting (df), every unordered pair of a posting's skills once
  (co-df). 5,344 of the 5,473 descriptions are stored cut short (they end in "..."), so skills named
  late in a posting are undercounted.
- `idf.json`: `normalize_idf(df, N)` (D-21) over N = 5,473 postings, for the 1,217 ids seen at least
  once; an id never seen takes `idf.unseen_default`.
- `pmi_edges.json`: 1,291 pairs mined (NPMI >= 0.4 over pairs in >= 5 postings, at most 20 per node,
  pairs where one id is an ancestor of the other left out; D-22) and 157 curated pairs laid over them.
- `common_words.json`: 3,845 words, each used in at least 20 postings, that resolve to no key;
  `resolve()` never fuzzy-matches them.
- Recompute against any database (reads only `DATABASE_URL`), from `match-engine/`; run the two compute
  scripts again after every `scripts/build_taxonomy.py`, then the gate (`tests/test_surface_forms.py`):

      .venv/Scripts/python.exe scripts/pull_corpus.py --env-file <path to .env> && .venv/Scripts/python.exe scripts/compute_idf.py && .venv/Scripts/python.exe scripts/compute_pmi.py
